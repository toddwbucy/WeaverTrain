## How it ran

- **Gemini Batch API, not opencode.** The operator set up a Google AI Studio account
  with billing. The batch price is half the live price.
- **Grouped requests.** Gemini 4 Argon has no explicit context cache ("Explicit
  caching is not supported"), and the rules plus worked examples are 14,074 tokens.
  One text per request would have sent that prefix 6,931 times. Each request instead
  held 20 texts with the rules as RULES.md gives them, and the answer was a JSON array
  of {id, output} under a response schema.
- **Live check first.** The same setup ran live on the 100-segment kit: 80/100 in
  round 1 (opencode sessions: 81) and 100/100 after one repair (opencode: 99), for
  $0.86 at live prices. Meaning review: 77 faithful, 21 minor, 1 wrong, 1 data
  (opencode sessions: 79, 20, 0, 1).
- **Turnaround** was about 10 minutes per batch, not the 24-hour worst case.

## What the checks found

1. **Short answers.** 14 of the 375 round 1 answers stopped after 1 to 4 of their 20
   texts, with a normal finish reason, so 236 texts were missing. The repair round
   asked for them fresh, and all came back.
2. **Invented markup.** A meaning review of 100 random converted segments found a
   stray `<h4>` at the end of one rewrite. A scan found 19 rewrites in 3 trajectories
   (T035: 17 with `<h4>`; T147: `<h4>`; T175: `<h5>`). One answer group had ended every
   rewrite with the tag, and the checker passed them because it did not look at
   markup. A new guard fails any rewrite that holds an HTML tag its input does not
   have (tags in the input are masked, so any tag in a rewrite was invented). Every
   part was re-checked, and round 4 repaired the 26 segments that then failed. No
   converted message now holds a tag that is not in the original.
3. **Integrity of the converted rows** (all 196). Only the agent's own messages
   changed. Issue text, tool output and tool-call arguments other than `finish`
   stay byte-identical. In `finish`, the `message` argument has new text. Other
   arguments stay equal as JSON values, but the spaces and the escape sequences for
   characters can change.
   Other source fields are unchanged. The `ste` field records provenance.
   No `[[Cn]]` placeholder is left.
4. **Common checker failures in round 1:** "ready" (94), "masked" or "mask" (58,
   picked up from the rules' own wording about masked placeholders), "direct" (31),
   "order" (23), "details" (19), and placeholders used twice (42). "order" and
   "details" are the known problem of the checker reading nouns as verbs.

## Meaning review (100 random converted segments: 90 narration, 10 final reports)

| | Faithful | Minor | Wrong | Data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Batch sample | 90 | 9 | 0 | 1 (the `<h4>`, now repaired) |

The batch reads better than the kits (77 to 81 faithful), because real trajectory
messages are mostly short, and short segments hardly drift. The drift that remains
follows the earlier pattern:

- certainty moves: "looks like the problematic line!" -> "Possibly, line 1895 is ...";
- detail is lost: "exact reproduction case" -> "test case"; "existing" functions
  read as new ones;
- a claim gets wider: "comprehensive edge cases tested" -> "all the edge cases".

## Bias to keep in mind

Only rows of at most 32,768 tokens were used, about 1 row in 10, so this data favors
shorter tasks. 4,000 uuids repeat in the dataset with different rows; rows are named by
line number, and one row per uuid was used.
